\section{Introduction}
Reinforcement learning agents are usually optimized against a proxy reward while
being judged, implicitly or explicitly, against a broader intended objective.
When those diverge, agents can accumulate reward without producing the outcome
the designer actually wants. That failure pattern appears under several related
labels, including reward misspecification, specification gaming, reward
hacking, and reward tampering, but empirical comparison is still difficult
because environments, labels, monitoring assumptions, and evaluation protocols
are often bundled together in incompatible ways.

\framework takes a diagnosis-first position. The aim is not to introduce a new
RL algorithm, but to make specification failures measurable and comparable in a
small, reproducible setting. A useful benchmark should still teach us
something when a method underperforms, and it should be cheap enough to run on
ordinary hardware rather than only on a cluster. Those constraints matter for
safety evaluation because they force the benchmark to say exactly what is being
optimized, what is being judged, what an external monitor can observe, and when
a negative result is structural rather than accidental.

We formalize a specification-diagnostic task as
\[
T = (M, R_p, J_t, C, M_{\mathrm{mon}}, \Delta, \Pi),
\]
where $M$ is the underlying MDP or POMDP, $R_p$ is the proxy reward used for
learning, $J_t$ is the hidden true objective used for evaluation, $C$ is the
corruption or tampering channel family, $M_{\mathrm{mon}}$ is the monitor
observation channel, $\Delta$ is the distribution-shift family, and $\Pi$ is
the policy or training family considered. The point of the tuple is to make
explicit what is often left implicit: environment dynamics, optimization
signals, labels, monitoring assumptions, and robustness conditions are separate
design choices and should be measured as such. In \framework, that
factorization is not only conceptual. Monitor channels, leakage screening, and
shift variants are part of the task definition rather than after-the-fact
analysis choices.

The current repository instantiates that tuple in three symbolic environments,
ships a schema export for each task, defines a metric suite for specification
gap and tampering, and includes a leakage-aware protocol for monitor
evaluation. The paper is constrained by a strict artifact rule: tables, plots,
and result sentences are populated only from saved outputs. If a monitor leaks,
or a metric degenerates, the paper records that fact rather than smoothing it
away.

The paper's methodological claim rests on three design principles. First,
\emph{factorization}: task dynamics, proxy reward, hidden objective, corruption
channel, monitor channel, shift family, and policy family should be explicit
benchmark components rather than bundled implementation details. Second,
\emph{admissibility}: monitor results should be reportable only when the label
structure is nondegenerate and leakage checks pass. Third, \emph{artifact
binding}: paper claims should point back to saved outputs, not to hand-copied
numbers or one-off notebooks.

Concretely, we make four contributions. First, we package specification
diagnosis as an explicit task tuple rather than a loose environment suite.
Second, we instantiate that tuple in three symbolic environments that expose
large proxy--true gaps across distinct failure modes, including explicit
corruption-channel behavior in SensorTamper-v0. Third, we formalize a
leakage-aware admissibility rule for monitor evaluation and report when the
monitor task itself fails that rule; under the current proxy-only protocol,
only SensorTamper-v0 yields an admissible hack-monitor problem. Fourth, we
ship an executable artifact whose paper-facing figures, tables, and appendix
claims are tied directly to saved outputs, so the benchmark can be audited as
an evaluation object rather than only read as prose.

This is therefore an evaluation paper, not a new RL method paper. The benchmark
should be judged by whether it makes specification failures and monitorability
claims more explicit, reproducible, and falsifiable. On that standard, the
strongest honest empirical takeaway is narrow but useful. Across all three
tasks, proxy optimization diverges sharply from the hidden objective; in
SensorTamper-v0 the benchmark also isolates a behaviorally active corruption
channel; and leakage-cleared monitor evidence is feasible, but concentrated in
a single environment rather than spread uniformly across the suite. That makes
\framework strongest as a compact benchmark and evaluation-methodology paper
for reward misspecification, tampering, and monitorability, rather than a claim
of broad safety coverage or a new RL solution method.
