\section{Conclusion}
We presented \framework, an auditable benchmark for diagnosing specification
failures in reinforcement learning. The core contribution is a reusable task
tuple and evaluation protocol that separate proxy reward, hidden objective,
corruption channels, monitor channels, distribution shift, and policy family,
and that couple those choices to explicit admissibility rules for monitoring
and to artifact-backed reporting. The benchmark instantiates that protocol in
three symbolic environments and ties analysis outputs directly to paper assets
through a reproducible local pipeline, appendix, and checklist.

The strongest empirical claim is conservative and useful. Across three symbolic
tasks, \framework exposes large specification gaps and explicit tampering risk,
while leakage-cleared monitor evidence is feasible but narrow, with accepted
results concentrated in SensorTamper-v0 because the other proxy-only monitor
tasks collapse under label degeneracy. In SensorTamper-v0, a tamper-off
ablation further shows that the learned proxy exploit depends on the active
corruption channel rather than surviving as a generic policy pathology. The
strongest honest version of the paper is therefore a compact benchmark and
evaluation-methodology contribution, not a general RL method paper. For a
benchmark-evaluation audience, that is a useful message: benchmark leakage,
label degeneracy, and reproducibility constraints are not side issues, but part
of what a trustworthy diagnostic benchmark must surface. If the paper is useful
to future work, it should be because it makes benchmark claims harder to bluff:
the task tuple is explicit, monitor results must be admissible before they are
reportable, and the manuscript stays attached to saved evidence all the way to
the final PDF.
