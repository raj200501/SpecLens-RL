\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built three small model organisms of reward hacking in which the true
objective is known exactly and the corruption channel can be scaled or switched
off, and used them to measure when PPO exploits a misspecified reward, whether
trajectory monitors detect it, and what happens when agents are trained against
a monitor. Three lessons seem likely to carry over to larger systems, as
hypotheses to test there.

\emph{The reward function does not determine the outcome.} The exact optimum
hacks from \res{opt-threshold-min}--\res{opt-threshold-max} times the default
channel scale, while in \PF{} and \GT{} PPO hacks only at several times that
scale, with runs that can disagree widely near its threshold, and where PPO
hacks in \PF{} and \ST{}, it is more harmful than the optimum. A reward
specification is safe or unsafe only relative to the optimizer that will be run
on it, across seeds.

\emph{Pooled monitor evaluations can hide weak monitoring within a policy.} When the policies
pooled in an evaluation hack at different rates (here mostly Proxy against
Oracle PPO, and in \PF{} also early against late snapshots), a monitor is
rewarded for recognizing the policy rather than the behavior. A policy-identity baseline and the
AUC within single policies separate the two, and they told apart two tasks in
which behavior monitors work within a policy from one in which they are much
weaker. We recommend reporting both.

\emph{A monitor's recall before it becomes a training signal says little about
its recall afterwards.} The same penalty removed hacking in one task and taught
agents to evade the monitor in another, a small-scale analogue of the
obfuscation \citet{baker2025monitoring} observe when optimizing against
chain-of-thought monitors, although here the evasion did not fool an
independently fitted monitor, which suggests held-out or ensembled monitors as a
cheap check. The monitor's own scores could not tell the two outcomes apart.
The known true objective could, and so could two checks that need no labels on
the penalized agents: a held-out monitor, and a test for inputs outside the
monitor's training range.

The environments, the evaluation pipeline and all results are released. A full
rerun takes about \res{cpu-hours-all} CPU-hours, and the report regenerates
every figure, table and number in this paper from the saved results in a few
minutes.
